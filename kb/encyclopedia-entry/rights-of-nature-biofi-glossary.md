summary: Rights of Nature is the legal-recognition framework treating ecosystems (trees, oceans, animals, mountains) as rights-bearing rather than as property, so that nature's own life-forms have a right to exist, persist, and regenerate, enforceable by people on the ecosystem's behalf; the ecosystem itself can be a named party with legal standing.
audience: ReFi BCN members and partners exploring legal/governance instruments for ecosystem protection alongside bioregional-finance mechanisms.
known_tensions:
  - Granting legal personhood/standing to ecosystems vs. existing property-law regimes built on nature-as-asset.
  - Who represents nature in court or governance (guardianship/trusteeship questions) is not resolved by the concept itself.
