type: encyclopedia-entry
page_type: framework
summary: A regional degrowth planning framework, modeled by the "Transició Ecosocial a Catalunya" report (CGT-Catalunya, March 2024), that pairs a biophysical/metabolic diagnosis (energy mix, material throughput, care-work share) with sufficiency-first targets across ten sectors — energy, materials & waste, ecological restoration/forestry, food & water, industry, transport, construction & housing, tourism, mining, and digitalisation — sequenced on a 2024-25 / 2026-30 / 2040 timeline and governed through annual citizen assemblies. Its distinctive move is treating demand-side contraction (four-day week, flight caps, device lifetime extension, land-use bans) as the primary lever, not efficiency or substitution alone, and explicitly rejects both business-as-usual and techno-optimistic Green New Deal pathways as unable to stay within planetary limits.
audience: bioregional stewards, ecosocial/degrowth researchers, municipalist and cooperative movement strategists
known_tensions:
  - Sufficiency mandates (bans on planned obsolescence, new open-pit mining, airport/motorway expansion; fuel rationing) vs. individual autonomy and market-based transition norms.
  - Union-led governance and campaign logic vs. the report's own proposed annual citizen-assembly monitoring model — unclear how the two decision structures reconcile.
  - Overtourism degrowth targets (cap flights, phase out cruises) vs. Catalonia's tourism-dependent regional GDP (~12%) and associated livelihoods.
