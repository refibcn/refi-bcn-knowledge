summary: Microsolidarity is a community-building practice for moving people out of isolated individualism into structures for belonging and meaningful work. Its core theory ("the Five Scales of Microsolidarity") holds that groups of different sizes are good for different things, and it operationalizes that through two core practices — Crewing (small, intimate groups of roughly 3-8 for mutual support) and Congregating (larger group gathering methodologies) — plus a leadership model ("Leadership as Hospitality") that reframes power/authority/hierarchy, and a stated trajectory "From Domination to Partnership" for transforming power dynamics at any scale. A structured "Developmental Pathway" takes practitioners from small-scale skill-building toward community-facilitation capacity.
audience: Community organizers, cooperative/DAO stewards, and bioregional-network builders looking for a scale-aware practice for belonging and governance culture rather than a funding or legal framework.
known_tensions:
  - Fractal claim ("patterns that work at multiple scales") vs. the practical difficulty of carrying small-group intimacy (Crewing) into larger congregations without losing the belonging effect.
  - '"Leadership as Hospitality" reframes authority but does not by itself resolve who holds structural power/resources in a group — the source does not address this tension directly.'
