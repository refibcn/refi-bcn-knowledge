  - BioFi's book "Designing and Implementing Bioregional Financing Facilities" (section 4, referenced by the glossary entry as the source of templates and case studies) — the immediate origin of the term as used in this corpus.
  - Participatory/commons finance traditions (community-owned, transparent, place-based governance of capital) rather than conventional fund structures.
toolkit_usage: Used in the BioFi Glossary to name the financial-capital-flow half of a two-institution model, paired with the Bioregional Hub (which carries social/intellectual/cultural capital flows). ReFi BCN's own bioregional proposals (see the bioregionalism article series and GG24 materials elsewhere in this corpus) use "BFF" as shorthand for this model.
adjacent_meanings:
  - Bioregional Hub — the companion institution; a BFF specializes in financial capital, a Hub in the other capital types
  - DAO treasury / impact fund — a BFF is explicitly community-owned and place-based, not just any pooled-capital vehicle
  - Bioregional Tithing — one specific funding mechanism a BFF might administer, not synonymous with the facility itself
important_distinctions:
  - a BFF is an institution (governance + capital-flow design), not a single product like a grant round or a token
  - '"participatory, transparent, place-based" are stated design commitments in the source, not empirically verified properties of any specific, already-operating BFF'
tensions:
  - community-owned governance vs the practical need for professionalized fund administration at scale
  - financial-capital specialization (BFF) vs the multi-capital ambition of the broader bioregional-regeneration strategy it serves
risks_of_flattening: Treating "BFF" as just a regional impact fund erases the source's insistence on community ownership and its structural pairing with a Bioregional Hub — a BFF without that hub relationship is, by this definition, an incomplete implementation.
