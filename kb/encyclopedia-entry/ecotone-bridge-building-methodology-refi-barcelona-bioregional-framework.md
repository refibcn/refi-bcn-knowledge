summary: 'ReFi Barcelona''s methodology for positioning itself as a deliberate "ecotone" — the ecologically-productive boundary zone between two ecosystems — across three named divides: urban-rural, global-local, and digital-physical. The core hypothesis is that boundaries between contexts are the most fertile zones for regenerative change because complementary strengths (capital/tech on one side, ecological stewardship/place-based knowledge on the other) combine into solutions neither side could develop alone. The methodology runs a four-step cycle — (1) Boundary Mapping: identify resource flows, value exchanges, power dynamics, and knowledge systems on each side; (2) Complementarity Analysis: find synergies between contexts; (3) Connection Design: build physical, economic, knowledge, and governance connections; (4) Pattern Evolution: adaptive review and diffusion of what works — and defines five success metrics (bi-directional flow volume, value distribution equity, participation balance, connection persistence, ecological outcomes).'
