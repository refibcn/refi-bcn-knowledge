short_description: '"Decreixentista" (degrowth) as used in the CGT-Catalunya "Transició Ecosocial a Catalunya" report: a deliberate, planned contraction of material and energy throughput, paired with redistribution of unpaid care work and union/community-led governance, framed as the only realist pathway that stays within planetary limits.'
source_traditions:
  - Décroissance movement (francophone, e.g. Latouche-lineage anti-growth critique)
  - Ecological economics / biophysical (social-metabolism) accounting
  - Ecofeminist critique of unpaid/feminised care work as an economic pillar
  - Spanish/Catalan union environmentalism (CGT, Ecologistas en Acción) — degrowth read through a labour-and-justice lens rather than a purely ecological or lifestyle one
toolkit_usage: 'Candidate template for bioregional sector-planning: pair a metabolic baseline (energy/material/care-work shares) with sequenced, sector-by-sector sufficiency targets and a named governance/monitoring mechanism, rather than a single aggregate emissions or GDP target.'
adjacent_meanings:
  - Décroissance (economic contraction as an end) vs. post-growth/wellbeing-economy reform currents (contraction as a side-effect of redefining success metrics) — this report sits closer to the former, explicit-contraction pole.
  - 'Overlaps with, but is not identical to, circular economy: circularity can remain growth-oriented (more throughput, just looped); this degrowth reading treats throughput reduction itself as the goal.'
important_distinctions:
  - 'Degrowth ≠ austerity/recession: the report frames its measures as care-centred and redistributive (four-day week, universalized agro-ecology, retrofit-led housing) rather than deprivation from an unplanned economic contraction.'
  - 'Degrowth ≠ green growth / Green New Deal: the report explicitly argues technological substitution and efficiency (its characterisation of a ''techno-optimistic Green New Deal'') cannot by itself stay within planetary limits — contraction of demand is treated as non-optional.'
tensions:
  - Radical sufficiency instruments (bans, moratoria, rationing) sit uneasily alongside liberal-democratic consent norms; the report resolves this only by asserting union/community legitimacy, not through the citizen-assembly mechanism it also proposes.
  - A labour-movement-authored degrowth vision (protecting/repurposing jobs) can diverge from environmentalist degrowth currents that prioritize ecological limits over employment continuity.
risks_of_flattening: Treating "decreixement"/degrowth as one unified program risks collapsing real disagreements between this union-led, anti-capitalist Catalan variant and other post-growth or "wellbeing economy" currents that accept market mechanisms and voluntary sufficiency rather than bans and rationing. The measures here (e.g. mandatory four-day week, mining-licence bans, fuel rationing) are considerably more prescriptive than most mainstream post-growth policy literature and should not be cited as representative of "degrowth" writ large without that caveat.
