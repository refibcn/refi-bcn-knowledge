title: Ecosystem Membership Hypothesis
type: concept-lineage
short_description: The claim that regenerative organizations thrive when membership is structured like a biological ecosystem — diverse participant types (analogous to producers, decomposers, pollinators, keystone species) playing complementary, non-uniform roles — rather than a binary member/non-member model.
source_traditions:
  - ecological systems thinking / biomimicry (forest-ecosystem role metaphor: primary producers, decomposers, pollinators, keystone species)
  - cooperative movement membership tiers (rights graduated by engagement)
  - Web3/DAO contributor-tier and reputation-based participation models
  - commons-governance literature (permeable boundaries, resource flows, reciprocity)
toolkit_usage: Used in the ReFi BCN "Multi-Stakeholder Membership Model" encyclopedia entry as the organizing rationale for the four member ecosystems (individual/project/network/supporting) and their cross-ecosystem integration mechanisms.
adjacent_meanings:
  - '"stakeholder ecosystem" in conventional multi-stakeholder governance (looser use, no ecological role-mapping)'
  - '"network organization" / "holacracy" (distributed roles, but not framed via ecological metaphor)'
important_distinctions:
  - '"ecosystem membership" (role-diverse, permeable, dynamic) vs "binary membership" (member/non-member, uniform rights) — the article''s explicit contrast'
  - descriptive ecological metaphor vs literal ecological governance (this is an organizational-design metaphor, distinct from the article's separate "non-human membership" proposal, which is about literal ecological representation in governance)
tensions:
  - metaphor risks over-claiming rigor — the "47% higher retention" and "63% increase in cross-pollination" figures are asserted with no cited methodology or comparison set (see accompanying claim-evidence object)
  - ecological role diversity vs cooperative principle of one-member-one-vote equality — graduated voting rights by member type could read as contradicting classic cooperative democratic norms
risks_of_flattening: Treating "ecosystem membership" as a validated, measured model (rather than a design hypothesis/metaphor the article itself names as a "hypothesis") would overstate its evidentiary basis; the retention/ cross-pollination statistics are unsourced and should not be cited as settled fact.
