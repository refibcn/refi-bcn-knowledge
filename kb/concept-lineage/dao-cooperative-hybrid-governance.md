title: DAO-Cooperative Hybrid Governance
type: concept-lineage
short_description: The idea of layering DAO (Decentralized Autonomous Organization) mechanisms — on-chain voting, tokenized incentives, programmable resource allocation — onto a legally-recognized cooperative's democratic governance, as framed by ReFi Barcelona's DAO-mechanisms article.
source_traditions:
  - Cooperative movement — nearly two centuries of democratic, member-owned organizations embedded in specific communities/territories
  - DAO / blockchain governance tradition — transparent, programmable, global-by-default digital-native organizations (Snapshot, Aragon-style on-chain voting, tokenized reputation)
  - Web3 mechanism-design tools folded in as implementation detail: conviction voting, quadratic funding, bonding curves
toolkit_usage: ReFi Barcelona's own four-hypothesis rationale (transparency → trust; tokenized incentives → participation; global connectivity → bioregional collaboration; technical innovation → new funding/verification mechanisms) for why a cooperative should adopt DAO mechanisms rather than choosing one tradition over the other.
adjacent_meanings:
  - DAO alone — a fully on-chain, often leaderless/tokenholder-governed org, with no cooperative legal wrapper
  - cooperative alone — a legally recognized, member-owned democratic org with no on-chain layer
  - platform cooperativism — worker/user-owned digital platforms (adjacent but distinct lineage, not named in this source)
important_distinctions:
  - The article insists economic rights stay tied to formal membership, NOT token holding — token accumulation is meant to create recognition, not disproportionate governance power (a deliberate divergence from plutocratic/token-weighted DAO governance norms)
  - On-chain votes (Snapshot/Aragon) are described as feeding INTO, not replacing, the cooperative's formal ratification process
tensions:
  - technical accessibility vs. innovation
  - formalization/mechanism vs. relational, trust-based cooperative culture
  - global protocol standardization vs. local (Catalan) adaptation and sovereignty
  - decision speed (on-chain) vs. deliberative depth (cooperative tradition)
  - infrastructure maintenance burden vs. the cooperative's core regenerative mission
risks_of_flattening: Treating this as "just bolt a token onto a coop" erases the article's stated safeguards (non-transferable reputation tokens, decay mechanisms against permanent power accumulation, democratic approval of every token system, on-chain votes as advisory-to-ratification rather than final) — the whole point of the model is to subordinate the DAO layer to cooperative principles, not the reverse.
