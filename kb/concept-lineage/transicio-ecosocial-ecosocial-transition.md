title: Transició Ecosocial (Ecosocial Transition)
type: concept-lineage
short_description: Catalan ESS-movement framing for a "just, democratic and eco-feminist" transition that fuses ecological transition (post-extractivist, post-growth economy) with social transition (care, feminist economics, migrant/climate justice, community governance) — deliberately broader than a purely technocratic "green transition".
source_traditions:
  - Social & solidarity economy (ESS) movement (Catalunya/Spain)
  - Degrowth / post-growth economics
  - Eco-feminism
  - Climate/energy-justice movements (e.g. Aliança contra la Pobresa Energètica)
  - Just Transition (broader international labour/climate framing)
toolkit_usage: Surfaced here as the organising frame of the Fòrum per la Transició Ecosocial (Barcelona, Feb 2023) and its "Futurs imPOSSIBLES" campaign; used to structure the ESS 2040 scenario-comparison exercise and nine cross-cutting strategic lines produced at the forum.
adjacent_meanings:
  - '"Green transition" / "energy transition" — narrower, often technocratic/market-based framings that the ESS movement explicitly positions "transició ecosocial" against.'
  - '"Just Transition" (international labour-climate usage) — overlaps but is usually framed around workforce/industry decarbonisation rather than the ESS''s broader care-economy and community-governance emphasis.'
important_distinctions:
  - Distinguishes a "Transformative Green New Deal" from a "Corporate Green New Deal" — the ecosocial-transition frame explicitly rejects the latter as extractivism with a green veneer.
tensions:
  - The source treats the ESS's role toward pessimistic futures (eco-fascism, corporate GND) as reactive ("brake and lever") rather than transformative — an internal tension in how ambitious the frame's own proponents believe the transition can be under adverse conditions.
risks_of_flattening: Compressing "transició ecosocial" to a synonym for "green transition" or "sustainability" erases its explicit eco-feminist and anti-extractivist commitments and its grounding in a specific 2022–23 Catalan campaign/coalition (XES, Coòpolis, Observatori del Deute en la Globalització) rather than a generic policy label.
